    The storm was visible. The danger was not.

    Satellites tracked Nargis across the Bay of Bengal for days. What many people did not get was a clear warning that the sea itself would rise and sweep over the delta.

    A warning only helps if people can act.

    People need information they can understand, trust and use safely.

    1. 01

      Make the message clear

      Warnings need to reach people in a language and format they understand, with a clear action to take.

    2. 02

      Build trust

      Research in Bangladesh describes how previous false warnings can make people hesitate.

    3. 03

      Make it possible to leave

      Transport, shelter access, property concerns and support needs can make evacuation difficult.

    These barriers are discussed in research about Bangladesh05; they are not a survey of current views in Myanmar’s Ayeyarwady Delta.

    A few numbers can’t tell the whole story.

    Use the complete forecast and local directions, not one rating or one sign from the sky.

    MYTH 01

    “A cyclone category tells me every hazard.”

    FACT

    The Saffir–Simpson category measures sustained wind only. Rainfall flooding and storm surge can still be dangerous.15, 14

    MYTH 02

    “The calculator gives an exact arrival time.”

    FACT

    It divides a distance by a speed you enter. A cyclone can change speed or direction, so keep checking official forecasts.

    MYTH 03

    “Only people on the coast need to prepare.”

    FACT

    Heavy rain and flooding can affect places far from the coast. Know the hazards and instructions for your area.14
